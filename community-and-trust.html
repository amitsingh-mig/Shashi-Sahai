COMMUNITY & TRUST / THREE RECOLLECTIONS

BY LISTENING.

An Aarohan team reflection remembers an early project discussion, relationships at Jamunwala Park and extended time spent with communities in Araku.

Based on recollections preserved on the original memorial. The accompanying documentary image is not assigned to a particular episode.

The Aarohan team reflection preserved on the original memorial describes three situations in which Shashi helped colleagues find a way forward. Each begins with uncertainty rather than a ready solution. Read together, the recollections show the importance the team placed on understanding people, staying involved and allowing relationships to develop over time.

01 / A TEAM RECOLLECTION

A question that changed a discussion

The team account begins with an opportunity to work with the transgender community. Its narrator recalls feeling apprehensive enough to suggest that Aarohan give up the project. Shashi’s response was to ask whether a decision could be made before the team had understood people’s lives in depth.

The recollection credits that question with changing the direction of the discussion. It was a request to learn before deciding. The account does not offer a detailed project history; it preserves the moment when an initial reluctance became a reason to look more closely at what the work might involve.

02 / A TEAM RECOLLECTION

A different relationship at Pustak Mahal

At Jamunwala Park, the same narrator remembers early difficulty in working with children. Photography, storytelling and patient relationships with the children and their parents form the centre of the account. The remembered change is described through the use of Pustak Mahal, a place children initially approached with hostility.

The reflection recalls children who had thrown stones later coming to read, take photographs and play their dhol. These are specific activities through which the narrator makes trust visible. The account credits Shashi’s persistence and relationships, giving the change its meaning through everyday participation at the centre.

03 / A TEAM RECOLLECTION

Time spent on the ground

The third recollection concerns Araku. According to the team reflection, Shashi spent nearly six months in tribal areas there, learning about communities and exploring practical forms of support. The emphasis is on the time invested in understanding local circumstances before deciding what might be useful.

This account is brief, but its place beside the other two is revealing. A project discussion, a children’s meeting place and an extended stay are different settings for a similar commitment: remain engaged long enough to understand what the people involved need. That is the working approach the team chose to remember.
