Two places to pause.

Pelicans over green water and birds above a city: a close reading of the archive’s two nature photographs.

This is a small collection: two photographs, two kinds of perch. One places pale pelicans above green water; the other finds birds on carved stone above a city. Their modest scale invites a slower visit, with time to move between feathers, surfaces, surrounding space and the shapes a resting bird makes.

01 / A CLOSER LOOK

The curve of a neck

Two pale pelicans perch on a log above green water. One bends its long neck toward its body to preen, creating a curve that changes the bird’s silhouette. The log gives both birds a shared line of support, while their pale feathers stand apart from the darker water. Begin with the whole shape, then notice the differences within it: neck, wing, bill, the narrow contact of a foot. The photograph contains a small action within a resting scene. Preening gives the still arrangement its own quiet movement, without requiring a dramatic event.

02 / A CLOSER LOOK

A ledge above the city

In the second frame, birds perch on pale carved stonework. A softly blurred city extends behind them, present enough to establish a setting without competing for every detail. The contrast is between a near surface we can inspect and a distant world that stays indistinct. Follow the stonework across the image and notice the birds as interruptions in its repeated forms. Their perches belong to the built environment, yet the frame draws attention to the living shapes using them. The city is seen from a different level, across a ledge rather than along a street.

A SECOND LOOK

What did
you notice?

Open each note, then return to the photograph.

01Curve and contact+

In Pelicans at Rest, follow the bent neck before finding the small points where the birds meet the log.

02Near and far+

In Above the City, compare the clarity of the carved stone with the softer city behind it.

03Two kinds of perch+

Across both photographs, compare the support beneath the birds: irregular wood and patterned stone.

There are only two frames here, but several routes through them. Move from bird to perch, from perch to setting, then back again. A longer look does not require a larger collection; it begins with giving these visible differences time.
