COLLECTION 05 / THE COMPANION PHOTOGRAPHS

THE CITY,
IN ANOTHER LIGHT.

A low orange sun and a tower illuminated against the night. Two photographs find different kinds of light within the built world.

Explore two frames ↓

What changes when the light changes?

The road and the tower belong to the city at different scales. One carries people through it; the other rises above its surroundings. In these two additions from the companion memorial, light gives each subject a new emphasis. An orange sky spreads its colour across an ordinary journey. Against the night, a lit structure becomes a sequence of shapes and bands. Neither photograph needs an exact place or hour to invite a closer look.

FRAME 01 / 02

A road beneath the sun

The sun sits low above the line of traffic. Cars and an auto-rickshaw share the roadway, while the elevated structure on the right draws the eye into the distance. The repeated supports give the scene a direction before the small vehicles carry us along it. Above that movement, the orange sky occupies an entirely different scale. Look first at the circle of the sun, then at the smaller lamps and vehicles below: the city remains busy beneath a broad field of colour.

Take another look +

Follow the converging edges of the road. Where do they take your eye before it returns to the sun?

FRAME 02 / 02

Colour against the night

In the second frame, the dark sky gives an illuminated tower room to stand apart. Its narrowing form and repeated architectural details can be read from bottom to top. Colour gathers on the structure instead of spreading across the sky. That reversal is the connection between the two photographs: in one, the surroundings supply the colour; in the other, a single building carries it. Move between the frames and notice how much of each city remains outside the light.

Take another look +

Compare the areas of darkness with the coloured surfaces. Which edges become clearest because of the contrast?
