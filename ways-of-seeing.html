Stories / 06A LOOKING GUIDE / THREE SMALL EXERCISES

Give a photograph another minute.

Three hands-at-work photographs become a practical guide to noticing a focal point, following a line and reading a wider setting.

There is no correct amount of time to spend with a photograph. But a small change in how you look can reveal something you missed. Try these three exercises with images of writing, drawing and working with thread. Each begins with a concrete detail, then asks you to change the scale of your attention.

01 / A CLOSER LOOK

First, find your starting point

Open The Open Notebook and give yourself a few seconds before reading its title again. Notice the first thing you register: perhaps the white shirt, the open pages, or the hand holding a pen. Now follow the relationship between those three elements. The bent body directs attention toward the page; the writing hand brings that attention to a smaller point. Try beginning from a different part of the frame and returning to the notebook. Does the image feel different when you approach the central action gradually? The exercise is simply to observe your own route through it.

02 / A CLOSER LOOK

Next, follow a picture within a picture

In A Portrait Taking Shape, find the large drawing sheet and then the small reference photograph. These are two images inside the photograph you are viewing. Move between them, looking at their different sizes and their relation to the artist’s hand. The act of drawing connects the reference with the emerging portrait, but the surrounding workspace remains visible too. Spend a moment at the edges of the large sheet. Then return to the marks on the page. This change of scale helps separate three things that can merge at first glance: the model image, the drawing and the work around them.

03 / A CLOSER LOOK

Finally, widen the scene

The colourful strands in Threads of Colour make an immediate pattern. Start there, then look for the seated man’s hands and the length of thread he is cutting. A display of colour becomes an activity with a specific action at its centre. Now widen your attention to the stone steps and the people around them. The work takes place within a larger public setting. Move through the sequence once more: pattern, hands, surroundings. Each stage adds information without replacing what came before. Try the same three-part movement on another photograph from the archive and see what changes.

A SECOND LOOK

What did
you notice?

Open each note, then return to the photograph.

01Choose a beginning+

In The Open Notebook, name your first point of attention, then deliberately choose another starting point.

02Compare three elements+

In A Portrait Taking Shape, compare the small reference photograph, the large drawing and the artist’s working hand.

03Widen your attention+

In Threads of Colour, move from the rows of strands to the cutting action, then out toward the steps.

Carry one exercise into your next visit: change your starting point, compare two scales, or move from a detail to its setting. The aim is not to solve the photograph. It is to notice more of what is already there.
