A city never holds still.

Yellow taxis, intersecting journeys and a football in rain: three photographs about the ways a street gathers movement.

A street is shared space, but it is rarely a single story. Someone is arriving; someone else is waiting; a game continues despite the water underfoot. These three frames move from the order of a building to the density of a crowd, then down to the small, unpredictable drama of a ball.

01 / A CLOSER LOOK

Yellow against white

The taxis announce themselves before the building does. Their yellow bodies form a bright, low band beneath the pale façade, where tall columns and arched entrances repeat at a different scale. Follow the vehicles across the frame, then let your eye rise. The rhythm changes from traffic to architecture: wheels and windows give way to columns and recesses. Neither layer cancels the other. The street is both a route through the city and a place with its own visual order. A passing vehicle makes that order newly visible for a moment.

02 / A CLOSER LOOK

More than one direction

In the crowded street, there is no single figure to follow for long. Pedestrians, cycle-rickshaws and cars occupy adjoining pockets of space, while market activity extends the scene into the distance. Look near the bottom of the photograph first, then work upward. Each new cluster changes the balance of the whole. The density rewards a wandering eye rather than a quick reading. What first appears to be one mass of movement separates into many small passages. People and vehicles become individual elements without losing their place in the shared street.

03 / A CLOSER LOOK

The game goes on

The rain photograph brings the viewpoint close to the ground. Faces and buildings leave the frame; bare legs, a ball and disturbed water carry the scene. The ball is a compact shape against a surface broken into pale splashes and dark patches. One suspended instant makes us aware of the movement around it: where the foot has been, where the ball might go. Those next moments remain outside the photograph. Here, a waterlogged playing surface becomes a whole arena, and the scale of the city contracts to the space between a foot and a football.

02Separate journeys+

In The Street in Motion, trace individual routes through the crowd instead of reading it as one solid mass.

In A Game in Rain, notice how little of a player is needed for the action to become legible.

Return to the first frame after the last. The taxis may now seem less settled, the architecture less dominant. Across all three photographs, public life takes its shape from temporary arrangements: a crossing, an opening, a game already moving on.
