THE PEOPLE
WE KEEP.

Rani Patel remembers nearly twenty years of friendship, shared purpose and dependable support through Aarohan and everyday life.

In her original remembrance, Rani Patel looks back on nearly twenty years of living and working alongside Shashi. Passages from that reflection also appear on The Long Look memorial site. Her account describes a relationship sustained by shared responsibilities, the freedom to disagree and the certainty that help would be there when it was needed.

01 / THE FRIENDSHIP

A shared purpose

Aarohan gave the friendship a continuing field of work. As a cofounder and longstanding contributor, Shashi helped support children, women and vulnerable communities. The recollection places emphasis on the tasks around that commitment: solving problems, finding a way through difficulty and remaining available.

Patel remembers resources sometimes being limited and circumstances demanding. Those conditions did not erase the purpose they shared. The account presents his contribution through accumulated work and time, rather than a single public occasion.

02 / THE FRIENDSHIP

Support without ceremony

The remembered situations are recognisable: an organisational crisis, a difficult decision, a personal worry, the exhaustion of a long day. Across them, Patel describes the reassurance of knowing that Shashi was nearby. Sometimes that assurance needed little conversation.

His usefulness was practical as well as emotional. The memorial recalls someone who made time when help was needed, without treating every act of care as something to announce. His presence could become so familiar that its steadiness was easier to feel than to describe.

03 / THE FRIENDSHIP

Room for difference

The account makes space for disagreement. Patel remembers differences that did not turn into separation, and a relationship that could contain several roles: friend, critic, guide and companion in a cause. It did not depend on choosing one description and holding to it.

There were also things Shashi kept private. The memorial acknowledges that even those close to him did not know every part of his experience. That uncertainty sits beside the recollection of a generous listener. Friendship here means sustained attention to another person, including the parts that remain unspoken.
