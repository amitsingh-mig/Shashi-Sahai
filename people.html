There is no such thing as an ordinary person.

In a crowd or in a quiet corner, the story begins with someone.

INSIDE THIS COLLECTION

A gesture, a glance, a presence

A face can hold the eye, but a portrait can begin almost anywhere: in a turned shoulder, an offered bite, a hand resting out of view. These nine photographs move between individual encounters and the closeness of a crowd. Some subjects meet the camera; others remain absorbed in their own moment. Viewed together, they invite us to notice how much a person can communicate without speaking, and how much every photograph leaves for us to wonder about.

Look first at the distances. A woman leans towards a child with food; a figure faces away into mist; a man looks through the repeated curves of an iron grille. Closeness and separation give these pictures their different emotional temperatures. Clothing, colour and the surrounding street offer clues, but they do not tell us everything about a life. The collection rewards a patient kind of looking: attention to a gesture, followed by room for the person to remain more than the story we might imagine.

Three things to look for

  1. 01

    Follow the gaze

    Notice who looks towards the lens, who turns away, and where your attention travels when a face is partly hidden.

  2. 02

    Read the gesture

    An offered bite and a lowered head carry different kinds of intimacy. Let the small action lead before the setting does.

  3. 03

    Keep the surroundings

    The rickshaw, the grille and the nearby crowd are part of each encounter. Look at how they frame the person without explaining them completely.

ONE FRAME, A CLOSER LOOK

A Shared Bite

The offered food links two figures through a small, familiar action. Behind them, points of light soften into colour. Look at the distance between hand and face: the photograph holds an exchange just before it is complete.
